A Baseball Announcer App for Teams Without a Press Box

Most youth fields have no PA system and nobody to run one. OnDeckDJ gives every player a stadium-style introduction from a phone and a speaker. Listen first, then read how it works.

Hear it

These are three of the 10 announcer voices, taken from the preview samples inside the app.

The Closer Full hype. The default voice.
The Veteran Deep, steady and calm.
The Firecracker Bright and snappy.

What the announcer says

The standard baseball intro is: "Now batting, number 7, Caleb Wilson!" You type the player's name and number, pick a voice and tap Generate. A few seconds later the intro is saved on your phone.

You can write your own line for any player instead. Add a position, a nickname or a hometown. Keep it short. The limit is 120 characters, and a walk-up only lasts a few seconds anyway.

The 10 voices

There are five male and five female voices, each with its own character.

  • The Closer, The Hype Man and The Walk-Off for big energy
  • The Veteran for a deep, calm ballpark sound
  • The Sportscaster and The Broadcaster for a polished broadcast style
  • The Firecracker, The Rally Caller and The Showstopper for bright, lively intros
  • The Warm Host for a friendly welcome, a good fit for the youngest teams

You can preview every voice in the app before you spend anything. One voice can cover the whole team, or each player can have a different one.

These are AI-generated voices. They are not recordings of a human announcer.

How it plays with the music

On game day you tap the batter. The announcer speaks, the player's song comes in underneath, and the music volume comes back up when the call is done. You do not mix anything by hand.

A player can also have an announcement with no song, or a song with no announcement.

More than at-bats

  • Pregame lineup. The app can introduce the whole batting order before the first pitch, one player after another.
  • Other sports. Teams can be set to softball, basketball, football, soccer, hockey or lacrosse, and the default wording changes to match.
  • Spanish and French. When the iPhone is set to Spanish or French, new players get an announcement in that language.

Prefer a real voice?

With Pro you can record intros yourself, right in the app. Some teams have a parent with a good announcer voice. Some let an older sibling do it. It is also a simple answer for any name you want said in one particular way. Our guide to recording your own announcer voice has scripts and tips.

How voice credits work

Making one AI intro uses one voice credit. Playing it back is free, as many times as you like, and it does not need an internet connection once it is made.

  • Free: 15 credits and one team of up to 12 players. That is enough to announce a full roster.
  • Pro: a one-time $9.99 purchase with 200 credits, unlimited teams and voice recording. No subscription and no per-player fees.
  • More credits: packs are $4.99 if you run through them.

What you need

An iPhone and a speaker loud enough for your field. An Apple Watch can act as a remote. There is no Android or iPad version today. Generating a voice needs an internet connection, so do that at home before the game.

If your league has rules about amplified sound during games, follow them. Those rules are set locally, so ask if you are not sure.
