High School Baseball Walk-Up Music: A Setup for Varsity and JV

High school players have been thinking about their walk-up song since they were ten. Running it for a school program takes a little more structure than it does for a youth team.

Start with the athletic director

A school team plays on school property under school and conference rules. Before anything else, ask your athletic director what is allowed: music during at-bats, between innings only, or pregame only. Your conference or state association may have its own guidance, and umpires may have a say on game day. Get the answer in writing so it does not depend on who is in the press box.

Write a one-paragraph song policy

Teenagers will test the edges. A short written policy saves you from arguing about songs one at a time. Something like this:

  • Songs are submitted by a set date and approved by the head coach.
  • The clip that plays must have no profanity and nothing that would embarrass the school.
  • The coach's decision is final. A declined song gets replaced by the player's backup.
  • Song changes happen between games, with a day's notice.

Because only 10 to 15 seconds play, review the clip, not the whole track. In OnDeckDJ you set a start point per song, so you can often keep a player's pick by starting at a clean section.

Who runs it

A student manager, an injured player or a parent volunteer. The job is one tap per batter in Game Mode, and the app keeps its place in the order. The phone has to be an iPhone, since OnDeckDJ has no Android or iPad version today.

Decide whose phone it lives on. If it is a student's phone, keep a copy of the lineup on a coach's phone too. The app can export the lineup as a file. The batting order and announcer voices come across, and Apple Music songs link up again on the second phone.

Using the school's sound system

If your field has a press box PA, you have two choices.

  • Plug in. Many PA mixers have an auxiliary input. With the right adapter cable for the iPhone, the app's audio plays through the stadium speakers. Ask whoever maintains the system which input to use, and test it on a practice day.
  • Bring a speaker. A strong portable Bluetooth speaker near the backstop is simpler and does not depend on anyone unlocking the press box. See our speaker guide for sizes that carry on a full-size field.

If a person announces your games live, the app can still handle the songs. Set players to music only and let your announcer do the names. If nobody announces, the AI announcer covers it: 10 voices, each intro generated from the player's name and number.

Varsity and JV

Set up varsity and JV as separate teams. A player who moves up is added to the varsity team and gets a new intro there. Pro allows unlimited teams and comes with 200 voice credits, at one credit per generated intro. That is enough for both squads with room for call-ups. Each team can hold up to 40 players.

Senior night and big games

Two features earn their keep here.

  • Pregame lineup introductions. The app reads the starting lineup in order, announcer style, before first pitch.
  • Custom announcements. Rewrite a senior's intro for the night: "Playing his final home game, number 21, senior center fielder Marcus Hill." Keep it to a sentence.

A coach or teacher can also record intros in their own voice with Pro. A familiar voice lands well on senior night.

Between innings

High school games have real gaps between half-innings while pitchers warm up. The app can play a playlist in those gaps and fade it out when you tap the next batter. Our guide to between-innings music has ideas for what to play.
