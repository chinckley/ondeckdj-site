Walk-Up Music for a Little League Team: A Volunteer Coach's Plan

You have a roster, a practice slot and a day job. This is the shortest path from a nice idea to music playing on opening day.

Three weeks out: ask the league

Whether you can play music during games is decided locally, by your league's board or sometimes by whoever owns the field. Do not assume either way. Send one short email to your league president or division coordinator and ask three things:

  • Is music allowed during at-bats, or only before games and between innings?
  • Is there a volume limit or a rule about where speakers go?
  • Does the other team's coach need to agree?

Our article on walk-up music rules in Little League explains who decides and what to do if the answer is no.

Two weeks out: collect songs

Send parents one message. Ask for each player's song, plus a backup, by a set date. Say that songs need to be clean for a family crowd. Tell them you only play about 10 seconds, so the best part of the song is what counts.

If your parents use iPhones, Team Link lets them suggest a song inside the app and you approve it. If collecting songs is more than you want to take on, pick one team song and let the announcer do the personal part.

Need a starting list? Here are 60 clean walk-up songs for Little League-age players.

One week out: build the lineup

This takes one evening.

  1. Download OnDeckDJ on your iPhone and create your team.
  2. Add each player with a name and jersey number.
  3. Attach the song from Apple Music or from an audio file on your phone. Set the start point so the clip opens on the part everyone knows.
  4. Generate the announcer intro. Pick one of 10 AI voices. The app says the player's name and number in a stadium style.
  5. Play each one back. Listen for lyrics and for volume.

The free version handles one team of up to 12 players and comes with 15 voice credits. One credit makes one intro. For most rec rosters, that covers the season with a few credits to spare.

A few days out: test with the speaker

Pair the phone with the speaker you will use and run the in-app Sound Check. Then tap through the whole lineup once. Do this at the field if you can, because sound that fills a living room disappears outdoors. If you still need a speaker, see our speaker guide.

Game day: hand it off

You are coaching, so someone else runs the music. Find a parent who is at most games and show them Game Mode. The whole job:

  • Tap the batter's name when they walk to the plate.
  • Tap stop when they are ready to hit.
  • Repeat. The app moves to the next batter by itself.

If you change the batting order, drag players into the new order before the game and the music follows it.

What it costs a volunteer

Often nothing. The app is free to download, and the free version is sized for one rec team. If you coach two teams, have more than 12 players, or want to record intros in your own voice, Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase with 200 voice credits. There is no subscription and no per-player fee, so there is nothing to collect from families.

OnDeckDJ is an iPhone app with an Apple Watch remote. There is no Android or iPad version today. Parents who join through Team Link need an iPhone too.

If something goes wrong

Speakers disconnect. Phones ring. If the music fails for a batter, let it go and fix it between innings. The kids care far more about the at-bat than the song. One missed walk-up will not be remembered by the next inning.
