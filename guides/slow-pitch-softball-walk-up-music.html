Walk-Up Music for Slow-Pitch and Adult League Softball

Your team has matching shirts, a cooler and a shortstop who thinks he is still 22. It is time to add walk-up music.

Why it works so well for adult leagues

Nobody on a weeknight softball team is getting scouted. Walk-up music is there for laughs and for a bit of swagger. It gives the quiet new teammate a moment, and it gives the loud one a theme song.

Adult teams also have an advantage over youth teams: everyone can choose their own song and nobody needs a parent to approve it. Whether the next field over wants to hear it is another matter.

Write the announcer intros for laughs

OnDeckDJ makes an AI announcer intro for each player. The standard line is the name and number. For an adult team, the custom announcement is where the fun is. You get 120 characters. Some directions teams take:

  • The made-up résumé: "Now batting, three-time office chili cook-off champion, number 14, Dave Kowalski."
  • The honest scouting report: "Batting seventh, and swinging at the first pitch no matter what, number 3, Jen Park."
  • The injury update: "Returning from a pulled hamstring suffered in the parking lot, number 22, Big Mike."

Pick from 10 voices. A serious, deep announcer voice reading a ridiculous line is usually the funniest choice. With Pro, a teammate can record the intros in their own voice.

Keep the clips short

Slow-pitch moves along. Many leagues play with a time limit. Some start each batter with a count already running. Batters do not take a long stroll to the plate. Check your league's format and plan for clips of 5 to 10 seconds.

Set the start point of each song to its most recognizable moment. Tap the batter as they leave the dugout. Tap stop when they are in the box, and the song fades.

Big lineups are fine

A lot of adult leagues bat the whole roster, so the order can run to 12 or more. Game Mode keeps its place and shows the next batter, which helps when the person running the phone is also due up third.

The free version of OnDeckDJ covers one team of up to 12 players. Pro raises that to 40 players a team and unlimited teams. That suits anyone who plays in a Tuesday league and a Thursday league.

Who runs it when everybody plays

On a youth team a parent runs the music. On an adult team, everybody is in the lineup. A few ways teams handle it:

  • The on-deck rule. Leave the phone by the speaker. Whoever is two batters away taps for the current batter.
  • The bench. A spouse, a friend, or the teammate who is sitting this inning.
  • The watch. If the team's organizer has an Apple Watch, they can run it from the field or the bench. The watch shows the current batter and has play, stop and next.

Park rules and good manners

Adult leagues often play in public parks, and many parks have their own rules about amplified sound and how late it can go. League rules differ too. Ask your league director before the first game.

Beyond the rules, a few habits keep everyone happy. Keep the volume to your own field. Stop the music when the batter is set. Think about who else is at the park before picking songs with explicit lyrics, since there is often a kids' game one diamond over.
