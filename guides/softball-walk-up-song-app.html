A Walk-Up Song App That Keeps Up With Softball

Fastpitch moves quickly and the dugout is already loud. Here is how to set up walk-up songs so they add to the game and never hold it up.

Softball is not baseball with a bigger ball

Most walk-up advice is written for baseball. Softball changes three things, and each one changes how you set up the music.

  • The walk is short. The on-deck circle is close and batters get in the box fast. A 20-second clip will still be playing when the pitcher is ready.
  • The dugout already makes noise. Many softball teams cheer and chant for every batter. Music should lead into the cheer and then get out of its way.
  • Game days come in bunches. Tournament softball often means several games in a day, so setup has to survive a long Saturday.

Set the clip length before you pick the songs

Decide how long a walk-up should last for your team first. For most softball teams that is somewhere around 8 to 12 seconds including the announcer. Then pick the part of each song that fits.

In OnDeckDJ you choose a start point for every song, so you can skip a slow intro and land on the hook. When the batter is set, tap stop and the song fades out. It does not cut off mid-word.

A simple test: play the clip and walk from your dugout to the plate. If you get there with a lot of song left, move the start point later or plan to stop it sooner.

Add the announcer

Each player gets a short intro in an AI announcer voice: "Now batting, number 12, Maya Torres." The song comes in under the voice and the volume comes back up when the call ends. There are 10 voices, five male and five female, and you can pick a different one for each player.

When you create the team, choose softball as the sport. A player who does not want a song can have the announcement on its own.

What it looks like on game day

One phone, one Bluetooth speaker, one person. Open Game Mode and tap the batter as she leaves the on-deck circle. The app keeps its place in the order, so the next batter is already on screen.

A few softball habits that work well:

  • Start the clip as she steps out of the circle, not when her name is called in the dugout.
  • Stop it when she digs in. The dugout cheer takes over from there.
  • Use between-innings music for the warm-up pitches in the circle. It fades on its own when you tap the next batter.
  • Save sound effects for big moments. A home run or a strikeout to end the inning. Not every pitch.

Songs, and where they come from

You can use songs from Apple Music or audio files you import to the phone. An Apple Music subscription is needed for Apple Music songs. Imported files play without a data connection, which helps at fields with poor signal.
