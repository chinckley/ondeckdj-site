Walk-Up Music for Travel Ball: Built for a Long Weekend

A rec team plays once or twice a week. A travel team can play five games between Friday night and Sunday afternoon. The music setup has to last as long as the players do.

Before you pack: ask the tournament

Every tournament and complex sets its own rules on music. Some welcome it. Some allow it only between innings. Some ban speakers because the fields sit close together. Check the tournament rules sheet or ask at check-in before you carry a speaker in. Your home league's rules do not travel with you.

Set up at home, not at the field

Creating an announcer intro needs an internet connection. Playing one does not. Once an intro is made, it is stored on the phone.

So do the setup on home Wi-Fi during the week. At the complex, with thousands of phones sharing one cell tower, you only need to tap.

The same thinking applies to songs. Audio files you import to the phone play with no connection at all. Apple Music songs may need a data connection, so test them with the phone in airplane mode before you count on them at a field with poor coverage.

Plan for the battery

A full tournament day is hard on a phone and a speaker.

  • Bring a power bank and a cable for the phone.
  • Charge the speaker overnight at the hotel, every night.
  • Turn the speaker off between games. You will usually have a long wait before the next one.
  • Use an Apple Watch if you have one. It works as a remote for Game Mode, so the phone can stay plugged in next to the speaker while you move around.

Guest players and roster changes

Travel rosters change. A guest player shows up for one weekend. Someone moves up from the younger team.

Adding a player takes a minute: name, number, song, generate the intro. If you are at the field and the signal is weak, add the player with a song that is already on your phone and generate the intro later, back at the hotel.

Batting orders change game to game as well. Drag the lineup into the new order before each game. Game Mode follows whatever order you set.

Share the load across families

The same parent should not have to run every game of a long weekend. Two things help.

  • Export the lineup. OnDeckDJ can send the lineup as a file to another coach or parent with the app. The batting order and the finished announcer voices come with it, so the second phone spends no voice credits. Apple Music songs link up again on the other phone. Imported audio files do not travel in the file and need to be added on that phone. A roster over 12 players needs Pro on the second phone as well.
  • Use Team Link. Parents with iPhones can follow the batting order live on their own phones, so they know when their player is coming up.

Be a good neighbor at shared complexes

Tournament fields are often back to back. Point your speaker at your own stands, not across the outfield at the next game. Keep clips short. Stop the music as soon as the batter is set. A team that is thoughtful about volume is far less likely to be the reason a director bans music next year.

The travel bag checklist

  • Speaker, charged, plus its charging cable
  • Power bank and phone cable
  • Lineup built and tested at home
  • Every intro generated before you leave Wi-Fi
  • A second parent who has the lineup file on their phone
