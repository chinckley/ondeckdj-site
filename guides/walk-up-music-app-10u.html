Walk-Up Music for 10U: The Year Kids Start Picking Their Own Song

Nine- and ten-year-olds have opinions about music. That makes walk-ups more fun and gives the coach one new job: saying yes to the right songs.

What changes at 10U

By 10U most players can name the song they want. They have heard it at a big-league game, in a video or from an older sibling. The song is now part of how they see themselves as a ballplayer.

That is good for team spirit. It also means some picks will have lyrics you would not play in front of grandparents. The work at this age is a fair, quick way to collect songs and check them.

Collect the picks in one week

  1. Ask for two songs per player. A first choice and a backup. If the first has a problem, you already have the answer.
  2. Set one rule and say it out loud. For example: nothing you could not play at school. Kids understand that rule straight away.
  3. Give a deadline. A week before the first game. Late picks get a team default song until they turn one in.
  4. Use Team Link if parents have iPhones. Parents can suggest their child's song inside OnDeckDJ and you approve it. It beats a 40-message group text.

Check only the part you will play

A walk-up is about 10 seconds. You do not need to review a whole song. You need to review those 10 seconds.

In OnDeckDJ, set the start point for each song and then listen to the clip. If a rough word falls inside the clip, slide the start point until it does not, or go to the backup song. Do not rely on a "clean" label alone. Edits vary.

For picks that are safe as released, see our 60 clean songs for youth baseball and 60 clean songs for youth softball.

Let the kids hear the announcer

Each player gets an AI announcer intro with their name and number. There are 10 voices. A fun five minutes at practice: play two or three voices through the speaker and let the team vote on one. Or let each player choose their own, since the voice can be set per player.

You can also change the words. A custom announcement can include a nickname or a position, as long as it stays short.

Game day at 10U

Kid-pitch games have more pauses than coach-pitch. There are more walks and more visits to the mound. Walk-up music fills the gap between batters without adding any time.

  • Tap the batter as they leave the on-deck spot.
  • Tap stop when they are set in the box. The song fades.
  • Keep clips near 10 seconds so the umpire never has to wait.
  • Hand the phone to a parent, not a coach. The app keeps track of who is up next.

When a player wants to change songs

It will happen, usually after a slump. Changing a song takes a minute and costs nothing. The announcer intro stays the same, so no voice credit is used. A simple team rule keeps it manageable: song changes are allowed between games, never during one.
