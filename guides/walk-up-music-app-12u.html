Walk-Up Music for 12U: Make It Feel Like a Real Ballpark

Twelve-year-olds notice the details. The right voice, the right five seconds of the song, their name said like it matters. Here is how to give a 12U team the full treatment.

Why 12U is the best age for this

For many kids, 12U is the last season on the small field. They are old enough to care about how their walk-up sounds and young enough to be thrilled by it. A little extra effort goes a long way this year.

The basics are the same as any age: one song and one announcer intro per player, one tap per batter. What follows are the extras that suit this age group.

Make each intro personal

OnDeckDJ creates an AI announcer intro for each player from their name and number. At 12U, it is worth going one step further.

  • Choose a voice per player. There are 10 announcer voices. The big hitter might want the deep one. The player at the top of the order might want the high-energy one.
  • Write a custom announcement. Add a nickname or a position: "Now batting, the shortstop, number 2, Eli 'Scoop' Ramirez." Keep it short so it does not eat the song.
  • Record your own. With Pro, a coach, parent or older sibling can record intros in their own voice.

Each generated intro uses one voice credit. Once it is made, playing it is free for as long as you keep the player.

Introduce the lineup before the first pitch

The app can read the whole batting order before the game, announcer style, one player after another. It works well for a home opener, a tournament game or the last game of the season. Each pregame intro uses one voice credit the first time. After that, replays are free.

Tell the other coach and the umpire you plan to do it, and keep it moving.

Let the players choose, with a short leash

Twelve-year-olds will pick current songs, and some of those have lyrics that do not belong at a youth game. Ask each player for a first choice and a backup. Then set the start point and listen to the exact clip that will play. If the clip is clean, the song is fine for your purposes.

For ideas with strong openings, see 50 hype walk-up songs and 100 baseball walk-up songs for 2026.

Bigger field, more speaker

If your team moves to a larger diamond partway through the year, the speaker that worked at the small field can sound thin. Put it up high, aim it at the stands, and test from the far end of the bleachers before the game. Our speaker guide covers what size you need.

Give a player the job

A player who is sitting an inning can run the music. The job is one tap per batter and the app shows who is up next. Kids this age take it seriously, and it frees up a parent. Just keep an adult nearby who knows how to reconnect the speaker.

All-star and tournament teams

If you also coach a summer or tournament team, set it up as a second team in the app. Pro allows unlimited teams. Each player's intro is made once per team, so plan your voice credits. Pro comes with 200.

Summer tournaments usually have their own rules about music and speakers. Ask the director when you check in.
