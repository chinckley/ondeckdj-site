Walk-Up Music for Team Parents: The Whole Job, Start to Finish

Somebody at the parent meeting asked who wanted to do the music, and you raised your hand. Good news: it is a small job once the setup is done.

What you are signing up for

About an hour of setup before the season. Then, at each game, a tap when a batter walks up and a tap when they are ready to hit. You will still be able to watch your own kid play.

You need an iPhone. You need a Bluetooth speaker, which the team may already own. And you need the coach's blessing, plus a quick check that your league allows music. That part is the coach's job, but ask whether it has been done.

Step 1: Get the songs from the other parents

This is the slowest part, so start early. Send one message to the team chat:

"I'm doing walk-up music this season. Please send me your player's song by Friday. Clean lyrics only. Only about 10 seconds will play, so tell me if there's a part you want. No pick by Friday means I choose!"

If the other parents have iPhones, there is an easier way. Share the team with Team Link and they can suggest their child's song inside the app. You approve each one. Team Link is free for them, with no account and no purchase.

Parents with Android phones cannot join Team Link, so take their picks by text.

Step 2: Set it up once

  1. Download OnDeckDJ and create the team.
  2. Add each player's name and jersey number.
  3. Add each song from Apple Music or from a file on your phone. Drag the start point to the part that should play.
  4. Tap Generate to make the announcer intro. Choose from 10 AI voices.
  5. Listen to each one. Check the lyrics in the clip.

Do this at home on Wi-Fi. Making an intro needs an internet connection. Playing it later does not.

Step 3: At the field

Arrive a few minutes early. Turn on the speaker, connect your phone, and set the phone to Do Not Disturb so your texts do not play to the crowd. Ask the coach for the batting order and drag the players into that order. Open Game Mode.

Then, for each batter: tap when they walk up, tap stop when they are in the box. The app shows who is next. If the coach swaps the order mid-game, tap the right player from the list.

Sit where you can see the on-deck circle and where your phone is close to the speaker.

Step 4: Have a backup person

You will miss a game at some point. There are two ways to cover it.

  • Hand over your phone. Simple, if you trust the other parent with it for two hours.
  • Send them the lineup. The app exports the lineup as a file. The other parent opens it in OnDeckDJ on their iPhone and gets the batting order and all the announcer voices. Nothing is charged for those voices on their phone. Apple Music songs link up again. Songs you imported as files need to be added on their phone. On the free version, importing works for a roster of up to 12 when they have no other team in the app.

Test the backup once before you need it.

Small things that make you good at this

  • Wait for the batter to start walking. Tapping early wastes the clip.
  • Stop the music early rather than late. Umpires appreciate it.
  • Keep the volume reasonable. The other team's parents are sitting right there.
  • Do not play anything while the ball is live. Sound effects are for after the play.
  • Let kids change songs between games, not during them.

Who pays, and how much

Probably nobody. OnDeckDJ is free to download, and the free version covers one team of up to 12 players with 15 announcer credits. One credit makes one intro.

If the roster is bigger than 12, or you end up doing this for a second team, Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase with 200 credits. There is no subscription and no per-player fee, so you will not be chasing families for money. Extra credit packs are $4.99.

The app is for iPhone only. There is no Android or iPad version today.
