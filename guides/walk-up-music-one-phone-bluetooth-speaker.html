How to Run Walk-Up Music From One Phone and a Bluetooth Speaker

You do not need a sound board or a laptop. One iPhone and one speaker will do it, as long as you sort out a few small things before the first pitch.

The night before

  • Charge both. Phone and speaker to full. Put a power bank in the bag.
  • Pair them at home. Connect the speaker in the iPhone's Bluetooth settings once, so the phone remembers it.
  • Check the lineup. Open OnDeckDJ and make sure every player has a song and an announcer intro. New intros need an internet connection, so make them now.
  • Update the batting order if the coach has sent one.

Where to put the speaker

Placement matters more than price.

  • Get it off the ground. On a bench, a bucket or a cooler. Sound from a speaker sitting in the grass does not carry.
  • Aim it at the stands, not at home plate. The crowd should hear it. The catcher and umpire should not have it pointed at them.
  • Keep the phone close. Bluetooth works best over a short, clear distance. A chain-link fence, a block dugout wall or a row of people between phone and speaker can make it stutter.
  • Keep it out of the sun and the sprinklers when you can.

If you want to stand away from the speaker, leave the phone beside it and use an Apple Watch as the remote. The watch shows the current batter and has play, stop and next.

Set up the phone

  1. Turn on a Focus mode such as Do Not Disturb, so calls and alert sounds do not come through the speaker.
  2. Set the phone volume high and adjust the loudness on the speaker, if it has its own volume buttons. That leaves you one control to think about.
  3. Close other apps that play audio. Only one app should be talking to the speaker.
  4. Open Game Mode and leave the phone on that screen.

Run a sound check

OnDeckDJ has a Sound Check that plays through the same path a real walk-up uses. If the sound comes out of the phone instead of the speaker, it tells you. Run it when you arrive.

Then play the first batter's walk-up once and walk to the far end of your bleachers. You should hear the announcer's words clearly from there. If you can hear it across the next field, turn it down.

During the game

The rhythm is the same for every batter.

  1. The batter leaves the on-deck circle. Tap their name.
  2. The announcer plays and the song comes in.
  3. The batter is set in the box. Tap stop. The song fades.
  4. The next batter is already on screen.

Between innings, you can let a playlist run. It fades out when you tap the next batter. The app also keeps the Bluetooth speaker awake between batters, so the first second of a clip is not cut off after a long half-inning.

Quick fixes

No sound from the speaker

Check that the speaker is on and shows as connected in the iPhone's Bluetooth settings. Stop and start playback. If that fails, turn Bluetooth off and on.

Sound is choppy

Move the phone closer to the speaker and clear the path between them. Check whether the speaker battery is low.

The speaker connected to someone else's phone

It happens at shared fields when another parent has paired with it before. Ask them to turn Bluetooth off for a moment, then reconnect yours.

A song will not play

Apple Music songs can need a data connection, and your subscription has to be active. Audio files imported to the phone do not need either. If the field has poor signal, imported files are the safer choice.

Nothing works and the batter is waiting

Skip that walk-up. Fix it between innings. Nobody loses an at-bat over a missing song.
