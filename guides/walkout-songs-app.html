A Walkout Songs App for Your Whole Lineup

Some people say walk-up song. Some say walkout song. Either way, you want the right clip to play for the right player at the right moment. Here is what an app adds.

Walkout or walk-up?

In baseball and softball the two terms mean the same thing: the song that plays as a batter heads to the plate. "Walk-up" is the more common phrase around ballparks. "Walkout" is also used for a pitcher coming in from the bullpen, and in other sports for a player or team entering the field or court.

OnDeckDJ uses "walk-up" inside the app. If you call them walkout songs, you are in the right place.

Why a playlist is not enough

Most people start with a playlist on their phone. It works for an inning or two. Then the problems show up.

  • Songs start at the beginning. The first 20 seconds of many songs are a slow build. The batter is in the box before the good part arrives.
  • You have to find each song. Scrolling for the right track while the next player is already walking is stressful.
  • The order changes. A playlist does not know your batting order, and it does not know you just made a substitution.
  • There is no announcer. The name and number are half the fun.
  • Stopping is abrupt. Hitting pause cuts the song dead.

What to look for in a walkout songs app

Whatever app you choose, check for these before you rely on it for a season.

  1. A start point for every song, so each clip opens on the hook.
  2. One tap per player, in batting order, with the next player ready on screen.
  3. A fade when you stop, not a hard cut.
  4. Name and number announcements that play before or over the song.
  5. Music from sources you already have. Find out which music services and file types the app supports before you build a lineup.
  6. Pricing you understand. Is it one price, a subscription, or a charge for each player? Add it up for your whole roster.
  7. It works at the field. Ask what still plays with a weak data connection.
